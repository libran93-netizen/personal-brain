## Short answer

**Multi-destination weddings as you described them (one couple, ceremonies in several places, guests following along) won't work as a standalone business.** There is a version next to it that could work, and you're unusually well placed to build it.

I'm working from what I already know about the market, not fresh web research. Treat the market figures below as rough, and check them before you put them in a deck.

---

## 1. Why the literal idea fails

| Problem | Why it's fatal |
|---|---|
| **Tiny demand** | Destination weddings are already a small slice of weddings. Couples who want *several* destinations, and can pay for it, are a tiny fraction of that. |
| **Guests won't follow** | Getting 150 relatives to one destination is already hard. Getting them to three is almost impossible. So it shrinks to the couple plus a few people, which is really an elopement product. |
| **Costs multiply, margins don't** | Each extra location adds vendors, permits, travel and risk. Your planning work roughly triples, but couples won't pay three times your fee. |
| **The law only counts one** | A marriage is legally registered once. The other "weddings" are ceremonies or photoshoots, which is a weaker sales pitch. |
| **It's a feature, not a company** | Any good planner (Vows Vacations, Motwane, Wedniksha, Meragi) will build a multi-city wedding if a client asks. Nobody owns the category because there isn't enough demand to own. That gap is a warning, not an opening. |
| **No repeat customers** | Each couple buys once. Every sale needs a new lead, so customer acquisition never gets cheaper. |

## 2. Who already exists

- **Indian destination wedding planners:** Vows Vacations, Motwane Entertainment, Wedniksha, Shaadi Squad, Tamarind Weddings, The Wedding Design Company, Meragi (funded, full-stack). WedMeGood is the marketplace. Taj, ITC and Oberoi push wedding packages hard. This market is crowded and decided by relationships. You'd lose here.
- **Abroad (elopement and adventure weddings):** Simply Eloped and Adventure Instead in the US, plus many operators in Iceland, Patagonia and the Dolomites. Destify books all-inclusive destination weddings. Adventure elopements are a proven category in the West.
- **India's mountains:** Couples already travel to Ladakh, Spiti and Kashmir for pre-wedding shoots. Triyuginarayan in Uttarakhand (the mythological wedding site of Shiva and Parvati) has become a wedding spot with state promotion behind it. The government's "Wed in India" push helps too. **Nobody credible owns safe, well-run Himalayan elopements and micro-weddings.** That is the real gap.

## 3. The version that could work: Himalayan elopements and micro-weddings

Turn "multiple destinations" into **one journey for the couple and up to about 25 people**:

1. **Pre-wedding shoot trek** (Spiti, Ladakh, Kedarkantha meadows): ₹60k–1.5L
2. **Mountain elopement or micro-wedding** (Triyuginarayan, a meadow camp, a heritage homestay): ₹3L for the couple alone, ₹10–25L for 20–40 guests
3. **Honeymoon trek** afterwards: ₹80k–2L

That's three destinations, sold as **one story**, delivered by a trek leader. It keeps the multi-destination idea and drops the parts that kill it.

**Why you have an advantage:** you already handle altitude, permits, weather, rescue, camps and local crews. A Delhi wedding planner can't run a wedding at 3,000m safely. You can. The thing couples fear most ("is this safe for us and our parents?") is your core skill.

**How to present yourself:** not as a wedding planner. *"Get married in the Himalayas, led by someone who has walked every trail."* Run it as a sub-brand of Blue Sheep, something like "Blue Sheep Vows" (just a placeholder). Keep the same bold, trustworthy voice.

**Rough numbers for year 1:** 15 shoot treks at about ₹1L, 6 elopements at about ₹3L and 4 micro-weddings at about ₹15L comes to roughly ₹93L in revenue. At 20–25% margin, that's about ₹20L gross profit. Worth doing as a second income line, not as a replacement for treks.

## 4. Risks of this version

- **It splits your focus.** This is the biggest risk. Blue Sheep's website isn't live, you have no email list and no paid ads. Starting a second business before the first one has its basics is the classic founder mistake.
- **It clashes with your busiest season.** Wedding demand peaks Oct–Feb plus spring. Trekking peaks Apr–Jun and Sep–Nov. The overlap is real, and you can't lead a Hampta batch and a wedding in the same week.
- **A wedding can't fail.** A trek can turn back because of weather. A wedding can't. You need backup venues, weather contingency days, and a medical plan for elderly guests at altitude.
- **Parents decide.** In India, parents usually make the wedding call, and many want a big fat wedding. Your real buyers are couples who are paying for themselves, have been married before, are marrying across cultures, or are holding a small "second ceremony" alongside the family wedding.
- **Photographers control demand.** Elopement couples often pick the photographer first. If photographers don't refer to you, you'll struggle to get leads.

## 5. A 90-day test with clear kill rules

**Weeks 1–2: Talk to people (cost ₹0)**
- 15 engaged or recently married couples aged 26–38, ideally your past trekkers
- 5 wedding and pre-wedding photographers who shoot in the mountains
- 3 destination planners. Ask: "Would you hand off the mountain part to me?"

**Weeks 3–4: Make the offer**
- One landing page and three packages: Shoot Trek, Elopement, Micro-Wedding, plus a "Full Journey" bundle
- Line up 2 venues (Triyuginarayan and one camp or homestay), a priest or celebrant, a caterer and a decorator

**Weeks 5–8: Spread the word**
- 3–5 photographer partnerships with a referral fee of about 10%
- Offer white-label mountain logistics to 2–3 planners. This is the fastest money because they already have clients.
- Reels from one styled shoot. Use a real couple or your own network to stage one and get the content.
